Empirical and molecular formula

The empirical formula is the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound. The molecular formula is the actual number of atoms in one molecule. For glucose the molecular formula is C6H12O6 and the empirical formula is CH2O: the ratio 6 : 12 : 6 cancels down to 1 : 2 : 1.

Sometimes the two are the same, as in water and carbon dioxide. Ionic compounds such as sodium chloride have only an empirical formula, because they are not made of separate molecules.

Finding the empirical formula from percentages

  1. Assume 100 g of the compound, so each percentage becomes a mass in grams.
  2. Divide each mass by the atomic weight of that element to get moles.
  3. Divide every answer by the smallest of them.
  4. If the results are not whole numbers, multiply all of them by 2, 3 or 4 until they are.

Example. A compound contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen.

ElementGrams in 100 g÷ atomic weightMoles÷ smallest
Carbon40.012.0113.331
Hydrogen6.71.0086.652
Oxygen53.315.9993.331

The ratio is 1 : 2 : 1, so the empirical formula is CH2O.

From empirical formula to molecular formula

You need one more fact: the molar mass of the compound, found by experiment.

  1. Work out the mass of the empirical formula. For CH2O it is 30.03.
  2. Divide the molar mass by it. The answer is a whole number, n.
  3. Multiply every subscript in the empirical formula by n.

If the compound above has a molar mass of about 180 g/mol, then n = 180 ÷ 30.03 = 6 and the molecular formula is C6H12O6, glucose. If its molar mass is about 60, n = 2 and it is C2H4O2, acetic acid. If about 30, it is formaldehyde itself. Three very different substances share one empirical formula.

When the ratio is not whole

Suppose step 3 gives 1 : 1.5. Do not round 1.5 to 2. Multiply both by 2 to get 2 : 3. A result near 1.33 or 1.67 needs multiplying by 3; near 1.25 or 1.75, by 4. An oxide of iron with 72.4% iron works out to Fe 1 : O 1.33, which becomes 3 : 4, the formula Fe3O4.

From an experiment

In the classic school experiment, a weighed strip of magnesium is burned in a crucible and the white ash is weighed. If 0.24 g of magnesium gives 0.40 g of oxide, the oxygen gained is 0.16 g. Moles of magnesium: 0.24 ÷ 24.30 = 0.0099. Moles of oxygen: 0.16 ÷ 16.00 = 0.0100. The ratio is 1 : 1, so the formula is MgO.

Pairs worth knowing

  • Benzene C6H6 and ethyne C2H2: both CH.
  • Hydrogen peroxide H2O2: empirical formula HO.
  • Phosphorus(V) oxide: molecule P4O10, empirical formula P2O5.
  • Butane C4H10: empirical formula C2H5.

Which values? This site uses the abridged standard atomic weights published by IUPAC (CIAAW), read on 12 November 2026. Your textbook or question paper may round them differently, for example H = 1 and O = 16, so its answer can differ slightly. In an exam, use the values the paper gives.
