Molar mass of oxides

An oxide is a compound of oxygen with one other element. Metal oxides are mostly basic solids, non-metal oxides are mostly acidic gases or liquids.

Oxides appear in the first mole calculations a student meets: burning magnesium, heating limestone, reducing copper oxide. The percentage of metal in an oxide ore, such as iron in haematite, is a standard question. Roman numerals in a name, as in iron(III) oxide, tell you the charge on the metal and so fix the formula.

This page lists 22 oxides with their formulas and molar masses, lightest first. The lightest here is water at 18.02 g/mol and the heaviest is iron(II,III) oxide at 231.53 g/mol. Tap a name for the full working, the percentage of each element and worked examples.

Which values? This site uses the abridged standard atomic weights published by IUPAC (CIAAW), read on 12 November 2026. Your textbook or question paper may round them differently, for example H = 1 and O = 16, so its answer can differ slightly. In an exam, use the values the paper gives.
