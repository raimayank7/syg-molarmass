Molar mass of oxalic acid dihydrate (H2C2O4·2H2O)

The molar mass of oxalic acid dihydrate, H2C2O4·2H2O, is 126.06 g/mol. One mole of it, which is 6.022 × 1023 molecules, has a mass of 126.06 grams.

126.06 g/mol

6 × 1.0080 + 2 × 12.011 + 6 × 15.999 = 126.064 · 14 atoms in the formula

About oxalic acid dihydrate

Crystalline oxalic acid holds two molecules of water. It is a primary standard, so this is the figure to use when a question says 6.3 g of oxalic acid crystals in a litre.

The working

The formula H2C2O4·2H2O has 6 hydrogen atoms, 2 carbon atoms and 6 oxygen atoms, 14 atoms in all. Multiply each count by the atomic weight of the element and add:

Hydrogen (H)61.00806.0484.80%
Carbon (C)212.01124.02219.06%
Oxygen (O)615.99995.99476.15%

6 × 1.0080 + 2 × 12.011 + 6 × 15.999 = 126.064 g/mol

With the rounded atomic masses used in many school books (H = 1, C = 12, O = 16) the answer is 126 g/mol. The difference from 126.06 is too small to matter in most school sums.

The water in the crystal

The part after the dot is water of crystallisation: 2 molecules of water for each H2C2O4. That water has a mass of 2 × 18.015 = 36.03 g/mol, which is 28.6% of the crystal. Without it, the anhydrous part H2C2O4 has a molar mass of 90.03 g/mol. Heating drives the water off, so a sample loses up to 28.6% of its mass. How this is used to find the number of waters is shown on the hydrates page.

Percentage composition

By mass, H2C2O4·2H2O is 4.80% hydrogen, 19.06% carbon and 76.15% oxygen. The largest share comes from oxygen. In a 100 g sample there are 4.8 g of hydrogen, 19.1 g of carbon and 76.1 g of oxygen.

Worked examples

Grams to moles. How many moles are in 10 g of H2C2O4·2H2O? Divide the mass by the molar mass: 10 ÷ 126.06 = 0.07932 mol.

Moles to grams. What is the mass of 1.5 mol of H2C2O4·2H2O? Multiply: 1.5 × 126.06 = 189.1 g.

Counting particles. 10 g contains 0.07932 mol × 6.022 × 1023 = 4.777 × 1022 molecules, and 6.688 × 1023 atoms in all.

Making a solution. A 0.1 mol/L solution contains 0.1 × 126.06 = 12.61 g of H2C2O4·2H2O in each litre. For 250 mL, weigh 3.152 g and make it up to the mark with water.

Questions people ask

What is the molar mass of H2C2O4.2H2O?

The molar mass of oxalic acid dihydrate (H2C2O4.2H2O) is 126.06 g/mol, or 126.064 g/mol before rounding, using IUPAC abridged atomic weights.

How many moles are in 100 g of H2C2O4.2H2O?

100 ÷ 126.06 = 0.7932 mol.

What is the mass of one molecule of H2C2O4.2H2O?

Divide the molar mass by the Avogadro constant: 126.06 ÷ 6.022 × 1023 = 2.093 × 10-22 g.

What is the percentage of oxygen in H2C2O4.2H2O?

Oxygen makes up 76.15% of the mass of H2C2O4.2H2O: 6 × 15.999 ÷ 126.064 × 100.

Which values? This site uses the abridged standard atomic weights published by IUPAC (CIAAW), read on 12 November 2026. Your textbook or question paper may round them differently, for example H = 1 and O = 16, so its answer can differ slightly. In an exam, use the values the paper gives.
