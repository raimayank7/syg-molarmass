Molar mass of barium sulphate (BaSO4)

The molar mass of barium sulphate, BaSO4, is 233.39 g/mol. One mole of it, which is 6.022 × 1023 formula units, has a mass of 233.39 grams.

233.39 g/mol

137.33 + 32.06 + 4 × 15.999 = 233.386 · 6 atoms in the formula · also called barium sulfate, barite

Open in the calculator

About barium sulphate

Barium sulphate is a dense white solid that does not dissolve in water or acids. Its formation as a white precipitate is the test for sulphate ions, and in gravimetric analysis the weighed precipitate gives the amount of sulphate.

Other names: barium sulfate, barite.

The working

The formula BaSO4 has 1 barium atom, 1 sulphur atom and 4 oxygen atoms, 6 atoms in all. Multiply each count by the atomic weight of the element and add:

137.33 + 32.06 + 4 × 15.999 = 233.386 g/mol

With the rounded atomic masses used in many school books (Ba = 137, S = 32, O = 16) the answer is 233 g/mol. The difference from 233.39 is too small to matter in most school sums.

Percentage composition

By mass, BaSO4 is 58.84% barium, 13.74% sulphur and 27.42% oxygen. The largest share comes from barium. In a 100 g sample there are 58.8 g of barium, 13.7 g of sulphur and 27.4 g of oxygen.

Worked examples

Grams to moles. How many moles are in 20 g of BaSO4? Divide the mass by the molar mass: 20 ÷ 233.39 = 0.08569 mol.

Moles to grams. What is the mass of 0.5 mol of BaSO4? Multiply: 0.5 × 233.39 = 116.7 g.

Counting particles. 20 g contains 0.08569 mol × 6.022 × 1023 = 5.161 × 1022 formula units.

Questions people ask

What is the molar mass of BaSO4?

The molar mass of barium sulphate (BaSO4) is 233.39 g/mol, or 233.386 g/mol before rounding, using IUPAC abridged atomic weights.

How many moles are in 100 g of BaSO4?

100 ÷ 233.39 = 0.4285 mol.

What is the mass of one formula unit of BaSO4?

Divide the molar mass by the Avogadro constant: 233.39 ÷ 6.022 × 1023 = 3.875 × 10-22 g.

What is the percentage of barium in BaSO4?

Barium makes up 58.84% of the mass of BaSO4: 1 × 137.33 ÷ 233.386 × 100.

Which values? This site uses the abridged standard atomic weights published by IUPAC (CIAAW), read on 12 November 2026. Your textbook or question paper may round them differently, for example H = 1 and O = 16, so its answer can differ slightly. In an exam, use the values the paper gives.
