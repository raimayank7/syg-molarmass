Molar mass of magnesium oxide (MgO)

The molar mass of magnesium oxide, MgO, is 40.30 g/mol. One mole of it, which is 6.022 × 1023 formula units, has a mass of 40.30 grams.

40.30 g/mol

24.305 + 15.999 = 40.304 · 2 atoms in the formula · also called magnesia

Open in the calculator

About magnesium oxide

Magnesium oxide is the white ash left when magnesium ribbon burns. Finding its formula by weighing the ribbon before and the ash after is a standard school experiment on empirical formulas.

Other names: magnesia.

The working

The formula MgO has 1 magnesium atom and 1 oxygen atom, 2 atoms in all. Multiply each count by the atomic weight of the element and add:

ElementAtomsAtomic weightTotalShare by mass
Magnesium (Mg)124.30524.30560.30%
Oxygen (O)115.99915.99939.70%

24.305 + 15.999 = 40.304 g/mol

With the rounded atomic masses used in many school books (Mg = 24, O = 16) the answer is 40 g/mol. The difference from 40.30 is small, but it shows why you should use the values your question paper gives.

Percentage composition

By mass, MgO is 60.30% magnesium and 39.70% oxygen. The largest share comes from magnesium. In a 100 g sample there are 60.3 g of magnesium and 39.7 g of oxygen.

Worked examples

Grams to moles. How many moles are in 50 g of MgO? Divide the mass by the molar mass: 50 ÷ 40.30 = 1.241 mol.

Moles to grams. What is the mass of 0.4 mol of MgO? Multiply: 0.4 × 40.30 = 16.12 g.

Counting particles. 50 g contains 1.241 mol × 6.022 × 1023 = 7.471 × 1023 formula units.

Questions people ask

What is the molar mass of MgO?

The molar mass of magnesium oxide (MgO) is 40.30 g/mol, or 40.304 g/mol before rounding, using IUPAC abridged atomic weights.

How many moles are in 100 g of MgO?

100 ÷ 40.30 = 2.481 mol.

What is the mass of one formula unit of MgO?

Divide the molar mass by the Avogadro constant: 40.30 ÷ 6.022 × 1023 = 6.693 × 10-23 g.

What is the percentage of magnesium in MgO?

Magnesium makes up 60.30% of the mass of MgO: 1 × 24.305 ÷ 40.304 × 100.

Which values? This site uses the abridged standard atomic weights published by IUPAC (CIAAW), read on 12 November 2026. Your textbook or question paper may round them differently, for example H = 1 and O = 16, so its answer can differ slightly. In an exam, use the values the paper gives.
